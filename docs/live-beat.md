# Live beat detection and snow shoveller

## Operation

Settings → Musik live im Takt erkennen offers an explicit Start/Stop control. The microphone can hear music from speakers, including playback on another device. Desktop browsers may instead offer sharing a music tab with audio. Capture starts only in a button gesture and is never restarted from saved preferences or on reload. Denied permission, unavailable capture and a share without audio cannot establish a measured live beat; confirmed catalog information or free dancing remains available.

Spotify's Web Playback SDK exposes playback state and control, not an application audio analyser. No SDK iframe, protected stream or Spotify credentials are intercepted. The optional analysis receives only the source the user explicitly shares. Display sharing requires a video track: the browser may show a screen-sharing indicator, but SpotDeck reads no video frames. Only audio is analysed, without recording, upload or playback of captured sound. A zero-gain sink keeps the Web Audio analysis graph live without feedback.

The default microphone choice is useful with speakers, not headphones. Tab audio support and protected-content capture depend on the browser and operating system; a silent stream cannot produce a beat. Microphone room noise, weak percussion and syncopation may prevent a stable result. BPM and phase are estimates, not a promise of sample-accurate Spotify synchronization. The user's beat delay, half/double speed, manual BPM and tap correction remain available while a live source is active. Mouth movement remains driven by lyrics, independently of BPM.

## Detector and lifecycle

LivePulseTracker analyses 2048-point spectrum frames and time-domain RMS within the existing single animation loop, at most every 20 ms. Positive spectral flux combines bass (40–190 Hz) and mid frequencies (190–2500 Hz). An adaptive 48-frame threshold and a 210 ms refractory period reject weak/noisy transients. At least eight onsets spanning 3.5 seconds are needed. A bounded search fits 70–180 BPM grids to at most 24 recent weighted onsets and accepts only strong grid agreement. The history retains at most 48 events / 16 seconds. Initial aliases favour the slower compatible pulse; half/double ambiguity is exposed through the existing speed control. No per-analysis network request or separate interval/RAF is created.

The grid tracks phase as well as tempo; updates preserve the current local phase before gently correcting it against observed onsets. Grid origins are rebased in whole two-beat groups to avoid errors from long browser uptimes and preserve left/right sway. Silence, suspended audio, track changes and long frame gaps invalidate the previous estimate. Hidden pages pause analysis and reset the grid, then reacquire when visible. The media grant stays active until Stop (clearly indicated), page exit, disconnect, or starting the owned-audio test source. Stop releases all audio/video tracks, disconnects nodes and closes the context. Epoch guards release late permission grants after cancellation. There is no MediaRecorder, saved audio or automatic grant reuse.

## Free catalog tempo and beat timestamps

Catalog lookup does not need a microphone, additional account, API key or access to Spotify's audio stream. MusicBrainz identifies the recording; AcousticBrainz can provide archived low-level analysis with a tempo and individual beat timestamps. AcousticBrainz stopped collecting new submissions in 2022, so its archive is useful for existing recordings and is not a complete source for recent releases. ReccoBeats is queried in parallel by the exact Spotify track ID, and its matched recording's audio features can supply a fallback tempo when no usable archive grid is available. ReccoBeats tempo alone does not establish where beats fall within the recording.

Recording matches check title/version, artist and duration, using ISRC where available; ReccoBeats results must additionally identify the requested Spotify recording. A live recording, remix or different edit must not inherit the original recording's beat map just because its base title is similar. Beat timestamps are checked for finite values, recording bounds, ordering, minimum separation, sufficient coverage and a bounded count. Invalid timestamp data is discarded without turning the fallback tempo into a measured beat grid.

The beat engine follows Spotify's anchored song position for catalog data. This lets a confirmed map follow seeking, pause/resume and Spotify Connect playback on another device without listening to that device. Between stored timestamps it uses the local beat interval, so tempo changes represented in the map remain represented in the animation. Only positions covered by actual timestamp data are marked as having a known phase. Before the first timestamp and after the last one, continuing the pulse is an estimate. A BPM-only result likewise supplies an estimated pulse, not measured beat positions; its phase starts from an assumption and may be audibly offset. Bluetooth, remote-device timing and playback-position latency may still require the existing delay correction.

Owned-audio analysis, a selected live source and manual/tap correction take precedence over automatic catalog data. Manual BPM and tap correction also work during Spotify playback without opening an audio capture session; returning to Auto restores the current confirmed catalog result. Stopping an explicit audio source likewise restores catalog information when available. A new track clears the previous track's map immediately, and an asynchronous result for an old track must never apply to its replacement. Provider cooldowns are independent: failure or rate limiting of one service must not prevent trying another. Confirmed results are cached locally with bounds on count, storage size and lifetime; no audio or Spotify bearer token is sent to these providers.

Primary sources: [AcousticBrainz API documentation](https://acousticbrainz.readthedocs.io/api.html), [AcousticBrainz project](https://acousticbrainz.org/), [ReccoBeats documentation](https://reccobeats.com/docs/), [Spotify playback state](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/get-information-about-the-users-current-playback). These services do not establish recognition coverage or timing accuracy for every song.

## Headphones and digital audio capture

For a supported desktop browser, selecting the Spotify Web Player tab with audio gives the live detector a digital signal while sound continues through headphones. The user selects a source once for each capture session; track changes in that tab do not inherently require another grant. The app must verify both the existence of an audio track and an actual non-silent signal. Sharing a tab on the computer does not capture Spotify playing on an iPhone or another Connect device.

System/window audio availability depends on the browser and operating system. Google documents system-audio presentation in Chrome 142+ on macOS 14.2+ and Windows 11; it should be treated as a capability to test, not as a guarantee that every Spotify audio path can be captured. Safari and mobile browsers must not be assumed to provide screen-capture audio. On a Mac without a useful browser capture path, the optional, open-source BlackHole driver can route app audio to a virtual input while a Multi-Output Device also feeds headphones. That requires installation and system audio setup; it is not automatic browser support.

`AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp()` and `outputLatency` describe the associated Web Audio context. They do not reveal the physical playback time of an unrelated Spotify app, iframe, remote device or every Bluetooth path. A detector therefore needs a stable audio clock plus the user-adjustable visual offset rather than a claim of universal exact synchronization.

Primary sources: [MDN getDisplayMedia](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getDisplayMedia), [Google system-audio announcement](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/12/share-devices-audio-when-presenting.html), [BlackHole project](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole), [BlackHole Multi-Output Device](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole/wiki/Multi-Output-Device), [MDN getOutputTimestamp](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/getOutputTimestamp), [MDN outputLatency](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/outputLatency).

## Snow child

When winter snowfall and automatic surprises are enabled, a pile at least 12 CSS pixels high can summon one small child with a hat, scarf and shovel. It walks from the right, scoops part of several neighbouring cells, carries visible snow, and throws it into a side bank. At most three trips run per visit, followed by a 25-second rest. The bank itself is excluded from the working area. Carried snow participates in totalMass; cancellation returns it to the floor. Existing 128-cell and 64-chunk limits remain. The child has no independent timer or animation loop.

The route and shovel stay clear of the cached album-cover bounds. When controls are visible, the child stays above the progress/playback area without drawing an artificial snow ledge. Snow is clipped out of that area; the stored floor remains available when party controls hide. A geometry change that blocks the route ends the visit safely. Reduced motion freezes the scene. Season, snowfall, surprise controls and Snow clear cancel it. “Schneehaufen & Kind zeigen” creates a small demonstration pile and lets the same automatic shovelling logic run. The original face artwork and lyric system are unchanged.

## Validation and limits

Run `node tests/shovel-beat.test.cjs` along with the six existing suites. New tests use synthetic spectral pulses at 72, 80, 97, 120, 142 and 172 BPM; they check phase against the known onset, jitter, a missed beat, silence and irregular transients. Media fakes cover explicit opt-in, zero playback gain, stopping tracks/contexts, late grants, missing audio, denial and page exit. Snow simulations cover autonomous threshold, gradual redistribution, mass conservation including carried payload, cover avoidance, bounded particles and cancellation. No real microphone, account or private audio is accessed by tests.

Actual microphone and tab capture with party music still needs verification on the user's device; synthetic tests establish algorithm behaviour, not recognition accuracy for every recording.

Primary API references: [Spotify SDK reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-playback-sdk/reference), [MDN getDisplayMedia](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getDisplayMedia), [MDN createMediaStreamSource](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/createMediaStreamSource).
