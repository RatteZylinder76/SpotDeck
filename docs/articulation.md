# Pronunciation-based singing

SpotDeck keeps the original white eyes and smile. The singing mouth now follows a deterministic sequence of pronunciation-based shapes instead of stepping through written letters with a per-word sine pulse. No new audio capture, audio upload, speech service or network pronunciation lookup is introduced.

## Planning and rendering

`ArticulationEngine` contains bounded German/English pronunciation lexicons and fallback rules. It treats German `ch`, `sch`, `ie`, `ei`, `au` and `eu` as pronunciation units, handles common English irregular words and silent-e patterns, and expands diphthongs into a vowel trajectory. For the illustrative phrase “ich bin hier, ich bin frei”, `bin` contains a B seal, `hier` carries a long I with a short vocalic-r release, and `frei` contains F–R–A–I. These are practical pronunciation estimates, not a complete phonetic model for all languages, dialects or singing styles. Automatic language selection uses a bounded sample of the lyrics; Settings → Mitsingen offers a German/English override for ambiguous cases.

Pronunciation maps to visual families for rest, closed lips, F/V contact, alveolar T/D/N, velar K/G/NG, H/breath, sibilants, rounded palatals, L, R and multiple vowel qualities. Voicing pairs intentionally share shapes; visible articulation does not identify every individual phoneme. Short I versus long I, E versus AE, O versus open O and U versus short U have distinct geometry. Their opening, width, roundness, lip curvature, corner tension and closure are blended independently. Most estimated duration is assigned to vowel nuclei, so a long note carries its vowel instead of repeatedly opening and closing for every letter. Neighboring cues prepare each other within small bounded windows. Estimated tiny separators may connect; supplied word or phone gaps remain silent. M/B/P seals are constraints, including during surprise/laughter expressions. The next vowel can release a seal continuously rather than inheriting a stale closed-mouth classification.

`MouthRig` renders four cubic curves within the existing 100×50 SVG mouth. Active cavities start from four genuine ellipse quarters with matching side/center tangents and curvature, avoiding the former rounded-rectangle corners. A more visible singing smile raises the corners while shortening the paired vertical side handles together. This forms a soft lower-lip bowl without double humps in the upper lip. The smile is applied once to raw targets; the trajectory interpolates them without applying it again. Rounded O/U and narrow F/V retain less lift and their phonetic opening. Aperture, mouth width, lip seals and tongue channels remain intact. The original idle smile is unchanged. A has a deeper jaw, I a flatter spread cavity, O a taller rounded cavity and U a smaller pucker. L and R have different profiles. F/V flatten the upper contour and lift the central lower lip into a visible contact; they do not acquire teeth. A small muted-rose tongue hint appears for L/TH/DH only. It is derived from the currently interpolated geometry and clipped inside the mouth opening. The original white eyes and exact resting contour remain unchanged. The resting stroke is 9; speech uses 4.2–4.5 to retain readable cavities in small faces. Stroke and detail channels blend continuously. Emotion cannot reopen a required seal.

The existing song clock and animation loop remain authoritative. Pronunciation/timing plans compile when the current row, source, language or lyric revision changes; frames use binary search and shape sampling. Current-row cues take priority over four-word neighbor context on either side. Pronunciation cache is bounded to 512 entries; compilation is bounded to 5,000 tokens / 50,000 segments. The existing normal/TV/reduced-motion cadence remains. Pause releases the last actually rendered geometry toward rest over 160 ms; resume blends from that actual resting geometry toward the current song-time target over 140 ms. Seeking/source replacement clears old articulation and pending blends immediately. Closed-lip cues still count as active singing, so the singing state does not blink off for each B/P/M.

## Continuous mouth motion

`MouthTrajectory` keeps an exact constraint schedule for closures, real silent gaps and phrase endings. Within connected non-seal speech runs it overlays nonnegative C2 gesture weights from a common compact kernel, carrying motion across short targets rather than stopping at every phoneme. The weights remain convex, preventing point overshoot and lip inversion. Long vowels still reach an exact steady plateau. No wall-clock history filter or additional animation loop is used; seeking samples the same deterministic song-time geometry.

B/P/M closures are prepared before the cue, held closed and released afterwards. A first closed consonant can move from the closed idle smile to the pressed seam without opening. Other first sounds use up to 140 ms of available lead-in to prepare their closed shape; opening, contact and tongue remain zero until the source cue starts. With no lead-in, the first large attack is duration-bounded and eased. Clear short consonant targets are retained; the overlapping gesture weights provide their coarticulation instead of first weakening each target to roughly a third of its form. H borrows its adjacent vowel context, and a rounded-vowel ending retains its width safeguard. Small lip-contact and tongue channels have their own eased phases, so a 65 ms gesture can retain a 41.6 ms peak instead of a 13 ms peak. These values describe a synthetic test schedule. F/V jaw and width follow the continuous speech arc. A separate eased lip-contact pulse moves the lower center and its two handles while preserving the central tangent and never crossing the upper lip. The pulse is bounded by real pauses and closed-lip cues; its onset and release are smoothed independently from the larger jaw. Seals still take priority. Teeth remain absent. Vowel-neighbor distance uses phoneme boundaries, so a directly adjacent long held vowel still influences a short final consonant; its distant midpoint must not disqualify it. Estimated tiny gaps can bridge; authoritative gaps close toward a quiet seam and remain closed. All these transformations include stroke width, so F/V and rest do not change line thickness abruptly. Per-frame expression bias preserves matching upper/lower curves and required seals.

A phrase ending no longer stretches a rounded singing mouth to the 84-unit original grin in 160 ms. Once closed, it settles over 220 ms into a shallow closed smile whose width follows the last articulated shape, bounded to 34–50 SVG units. That relaxed smile holds for 1,000 ms, then recovers the exact original resting geometry and stroke over 1,600 ms if the silence continues. Short gaps retain the closed articulated forms instead of invoking the full grin. Re-entry blends the still-evolving recovery into the next closed phoneme preparation, preserving position, velocity and acceleration even when the next phrase begins halfway through recovery. All timing comes from absolute song positions, with no frame history or extra animation loop. Recompiling neighboring lyric rows therefore preserves the same recovery at a shared song position.

The pure helpers `recoveryFrom(rendered)` and `sampleRecovery(recovery, elapsedMs)` expose the same closed relaxation geometry for other release paths. The first helper deliberately projects to a closed seam; a caller releasing an open mouth must animate that initial closure before sampling this recovery. The 220/1,000/1,600 ms durations are visual starting values, not measurements of a singer's physiology or the actual recording. The character's original broad smile remains its exact long-term resting face.

Regression checks compare actual sampled motion, including unnecessary stationary spans in short consonants, rather than counting only easing phases. They also check point movement at 30/60 Hz, boundary position, stroke, velocity and acceleration. Separate contact/detail tests check visibility across different frame offsets. These synthetic schedules do not establish physical-device FPS or alignment to a singer's recording.

## Timing and honest quality labels

Empty timestamped LRC entries are preserved as the preceding visible row’s end, without creating blank UI rows. Consecutive and repeated timestamps remain bounded. This prevents explicit instrumental pauses from being discarded. For line-only rows with no supplied end, a smooth conservative phrase-span estimate replaces the former discontinuity at 14 seconds. Weak function words get modestly reduced weights; inferred consonants have short readable durations when space permits. Supplied word/phone boundaries and isolated sustained vocal words retain their full intervals. These estimates can still differ from a real singer.

Line-only LRC still supplies line times. Words and their internal sounds are estimated, even when the displayed line is correct. Supplied word starts/ends are used directly; missing ends are inferred and marked as such. Word-synchronized input does not imply phoneme accuracy. Optional supplied phone cues preserve their own boundaries; mixed files are identified as mixed rather than claiming every sound was measured. Diagnostics and the settings note distinguish these levels.

Matching lyric sources may improve in the background from line times to word starts or complete word intervals. The current pronunciation and trajectory planners then use the supplied boundaries without stretching the word across a real following pause. A complete word interval still does not measure the timing of its internal consonants, vowels or note changes. See [automatic timing selection and playback-clock checks](lyrics-sync.md).

Long gaps following a line can contain instrumental music. Without audio, exact placement of every held note or internal pause remains unknown. Pronunciation rules also cannot resolve every English homograph, foreign word or regional performance. Very short source cues may fall between display frames at 30 Hz. Those limits are not fixed by a higher frame rate or a larger viseme set.

## Optional local phone cues

The existing version-1 Lyricsfile format accepts `metadata.language`, line `language` and word `language` (`de`/`en`). A word may additionally carry `phones` containing `phone`, `start_ms` and `end_ms`. Times are absolute song milliseconds. A word end is required when phone cues are present. Phone times must be finite nonnegative integers, ordered, nonoverlapping and inside the word. Internal labels and a bounded set of IPA aliases are accepted. Unknown phones, oversized lists and invalid boundaries are rejected. Limits are 512 phone entries per word and 50,000 entries per file. This extension preserves existing line/word-only files.

[articulation-example.lyricsfile.yaml](articulation-example.lyricsfile.yaml) contains an illustrative manual schedule for the user's test phrase. It is a format/example fixture, not an alignment of a commercial recording. A later audio-assisted system could generate such schedules for suitable source audio, but that analysis is not part of this change. The existing explicitly started live beat detector remains separate.

## Comparison and verification

[`mouth-preview.html`](../mouth-preview.html) compares the previous gently smiling implementation against the more expressive continuous speech flow. Both sides use the same dark cavity, white contour and subtle tongue. The new side embeds the identical four production modules, including `MouthSvgRenderer`, so a separate preview style cannot conceal a player-only fill bug. The renderer applies a dark mouth cavity, the original white contour and a clipped muted tongue directly; no teeth layer exists. The silent explicit-play demo offers word examples, synthetic “love is gone” and connected “we are free tonight” sequences, 21 isolated sound targets, pause, scrub, phase jumps and custom text. Both sides of the connected example use identical supplied phoneme times, so timing estimates cannot conceal differences in motion. No commercial recording is analysed or aligned by the demo, no Spotify session is needed, and the actual player is unaffected.

Run all twenty integration suites. `articulation.test.cjs` covers pronunciation, vowel holds, coarticulation, genuine gaps, timing provenance, hard seals over expressions, readable narrow apertures, imported phone validation, tiny durations, deterministic poses, cache/resource bounds, source/seek/pause lifecycle and actual 30/60 Hz frame updates. `mouth-continuity.test.cjs` covers rendered point/stroke continuity, velocity and acceleration joins, real/tiny gaps, seals, held vowels with short final consonants, delayed width recovery, bounds, deterministic seeks, line recompilation through silence, pause/resume and frame-to-frame movement. `mouth-preview.test.cjs` verifies that the comparison embeds the identical production modules and exercises play/pause/scrub/jumps, presets, safe custom text, completion and hidden-page stop. `mouth-renderer.test.cjs` checks the real DOM paint, clipping, tongue visibility, absence of teeth, source-marked pauses and single-loop 30/60 Hz updates. `word-boundary-singing.test.cjs` checks supplied versus inferred word ends, vowel holds, genuine gaps, source-quality labels and a bounded pronunciation repair for Cyrillic e inside otherwise Latin words. The separate lyric-quality and playback-clock suites verify source upgrades and transport timing. Procedural pose rendering and automated timing tests do not establish physical-device frame rate or exact alignment for every Spotify recording.
